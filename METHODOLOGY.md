# Documentation Process

## How I Use AI

I use AI throughout my work to help me research topics, understand issues during investigations and structure my write ups. When I come across something I do not understand, I take the time to learn it well enough to solve the problem, rather than allowing AI to solve the problem for me.

I take responsibility for what is published here and my goal is to make this portfolio an accurate representation of what I've done myself. 

---

## Review Process

Before publishing, I review each document and look for:

- **Accuracy:** technical details, versions and configuration details that do not match my actual results.
- **Sensitive information:** details that could reveal specifics about my network or security tooling (hostnames, subnets, service identifiers).
- **Scope:** anything that overstates what I actually did.

My process is not perfect and I am constantly learning more about how to work effectively with AI.

---

## Examples of Issues Caught

Some of these relate to documents that have since been removed from the portfolio.

- A hostname containing a unique configuration ID was included in a document. Removed.
- The model attributed security research work to me. Corrected to reflect the real scope.
- Steps I had not yet completed were written up as if I had. Removed.
- A write up about a real world compromise misidentified where the compromise occurred. Corrected.
- An investigation report explained the cause of an alert using a mechanism the logs did not support. The explanation came from AI and I could not explain it myself. Removed.

---

## Risks

AI tools can and do hallucinate and provide confident answers that are entirely made up and wrong. They also tend to be sycophantic, agreeing with the user and inflating the descriptions of work performed. In a security context, acting on incorrect information could have real consequences. I believe AI can be used responsibly and part of that responsible use is carefully reviewing the output.
